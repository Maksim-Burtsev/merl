### Fixed

- `d` in Common Lisp, Emacs Lisp and Scheme on a name bound again by `dolist`, `dotimes`, `do`,
  `destructuring-bind`, `multiple-value-bind`, `seq-let`, `with-slots`, `receive`, `guard`,
  `cl-flet`, `cl-labels`, `macrolet` or `symbol-macrolet` stays on that binding instead of jumping
  to the top-level function of the same name (#1401)
- `d` in Racket, Scheme, Clojure, Emacs Lisp and Common Lisp on a name bound again by a `for`
  clause, an internal `define` or `define-values`, `case-lambda`, a rest-symbol `lambda`, `as->`,
  `catch`, a `:let` of `for`/`doseq`, a `reify` or `defrecord` method, `if-let`, `cl-loop`,
  `condition-case` or `handler-case` stays on that binding instead of jumping to the top-level
  function of the same name; the else branch of `if-let` no longer reads the name as bound (#1401)
- `d` in Racket and Emacs Lisp on a name bound by a `match` or `match-lambda` clause, by
  `match-define`, or by a comma-led atom of a `pcase-let` backquote pattern (and a plain
  `pcase-let` binding) stays on that binding instead of jumping to the top-level function of the
  same name; constructors, quoted symbols and the expressions of `?`, `app` and `==` stay unbound.
  A `match` is read this way only in a file with Racket's `match` (`#lang racket` or a
  `racket/match` require) and no `match` of its own, such as SICP's matcher (#1401)
- `d` in Racket, Scheme, Emacs Lisp and Common Lisp on a name inside the value of a `let`,
  `pcase-let` or `flet` entry no longer lands on a name the same form binds: those are bound only
  in the body (#1401)

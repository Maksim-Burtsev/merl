### Fixed

- `d` in Scala on a name a renaming import binds (`import shop.cups.{Mug => Cup}`, `{Mug as Cup}`) lands on the original class, and a method called on it (`new Cup().pour()`, `val a = Cup(); a.pour()`) on the original's method, instead of on another package's namesake `class Cup`; in Kotlin a method called on a renamed import's constructor (`Cup().pour()`) lands on the original's method instead of a picker; in Elixir `d` on the alias of `alias Shop.Cups, as: C` lands on `defmodule Shop.Cups` instead of a project `defmodule C` (#1466)
- `d` in C# follows a renaming `using Cup = Shop.Cups.Mug;`: `new Cup()`, `Cup` and a receiver built by `new Cup()` land on `Mug`, and a `class Cup` of an enclosing namespace, which hides the alias, no longer sends `new Cup().Pour()` to `Mug.Pour` (#1466)
- `d` in Kotlin on a method called through a typealias (`typealias Kettle = shop.cups.Mug`, then `Kettle().pour()` or `val k = Kettle(); k.pour()`) lands on the aliased class's method instead of a picker (#1466)
- `d` in Go on a method or field of a composite literal (`Teapot{}.Pour()`, `c.Mug{}.Pour()`, and through a type alias, `type Cup = c.Mug` then `Cup{}.Pour()`) lands on that type's member instead of a picker of every namesake (#1466)

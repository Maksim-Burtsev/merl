### Fixed

- `d` on a bare case of a C# switch-expression arm (`Rook => 1,` after a `using static`) no longer jumps to the word itself as if it were a lambda parameter (#1448)
- `d` in Java on a bare enum case lands on the case of the enum the code means: a `case ROOK:` or `case ROOK ->` in a `switch` on a variable whose enum type is known, and a plain `ROOK` after `import static aviary.Corvid.*;` or `import static aviary.Corvid.ROOK;`, where it said "no definition"; a case label in a switch on a type merl cannot read, a name the enclosing enum declares itself, and a static import of an enum outside the project still do not jump (#1448)
- `d` in Kotlin on a bare enum case (`LARK ->` in a `when`, or a plain `LARK`) after `import gambit.Warbler.LARK` or `import gambit.Caper.*` lands on that enum's case, the explicit import winning over a star import of a namesake's enum, where it said "no definition" (#1448)
- `d` in Rust on a variant after `use crate::chessman::Chessman::{self, *};` lands on it as after `use crate::chessman::Chessman::*;`, where it said "no definition" (#1448)
- `d` in C# on a bare enum case after `using static Shop.Rookery.Corvid;` (`case Rook:`, a switch-expression arm, a plain `Rook`) lands on that enum's case, where it said "no definition"; a member or parameter of the same name keeps its answer (#1448)
- `d` in Swift on `.rook` in a `case` of a `switch` on a local or parameter, or after `x ==` / `x !=`, lands on the case of that variable's written type instead of offering every enum's `rook` (#1448)
- `d` in Swift on `.rook` passed to a top-level function (`perch(.rook)`, `nest(depth: 1, piece: .rook)`) lands on the case of the parameter's written type instead of offering every enum's `rook`; a method or a local closure of the same name keeps the picker (#1448)
- `d` in Dart on `Corvid.rook` lands on Corvid's `rook` when the enum is declared on one line (`enum Corvid { raven, rook }`), instead of offering every enum's `rook` (#1448)
- `d` in Dart on a dot shorthand compared with a typed variable (`bird == .rook`, `piece != .rook`) lands on the case of that variable's enum instead of offering every enum's `rook` (#1448)
- `d` in Scala on a bare enum case after `import Hound.*`, `import Hound._` or `import Hound.Setter` (`case Setter =>`, a plain `Setter`) lands on that enum's case instead of offering every enum's `Setter`; two wildcard imports that both hold the case keep the picker (#1448)

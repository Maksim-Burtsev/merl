### Fixed

- `d` in Groovy and Jenkins pipelines on a name bound again by a local earlier on its line, a typed closure parameter or `def (a, x) = …` lands on that binding, not on the outer field, and a closure closed before the cursor no longer hides the outer name (#1398)
- `d` in Java and Kotlin on a name after a lambda, a `for`, an `if (… instanceof T x)`, a `catch` or a `try (…)` closed earlier on its line no longer lands on the closed binding: it goes to the outer field, and a method called on that name no longer jumps to a member of the closed binding's type (#1398)
- `d` in Java on a name after one of these, earlier on its line, goes to the outer field instead of the pattern's type: a closed `for` or `if` with a `/* … */` comment in the line, `o instanceof T x` in a statement that is no `if`, `if (o instanceof T x) … else …` whose `else` finishes normally, and a `switch` whose `case T x` arm has ended (#1398)
- `d` in Java on a local declared after a comma (`Mug d = new Mug(), c = d;`), or in an earlier `case …:` group of the same `switch`, lands on that local instead of an outer field of the same name, and a method called on it goes to the local's type; a method called on a `case T x ->` pattern goes to `T`'s method instead of opening a picker (#1398)
- `d` in Java on a local declared after a comma in a `for` header (`for (Mug d = …, c = d; …)`), or after a comma on a continuation line (`Mug d = new Mug(),` / `c = d;`), lands on that local instead of an outer field of the same name; and `d` in a Groovy script or Jenkinsfile on a name assigned at its top level (`x = 1`, then `println x`) lands on that assignment (#1398)

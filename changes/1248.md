### Fixed

- `d` in C++ on the name of a constructor or destructor defined inside its class (`Harness() : constructor_(nullptr) {}`) stays on that definition, as on a method defined in its class, instead of jumping to the `class` line (#1248)
- `d` in C++ on the name of a constructor defined out of its class (`Key::Key(int a, int b) {}`) lands on the declaration of that overload in the class instead of another overload's definition, and on a delegating call (`Steeper() : Steeper(500) {}`) on the one constructor taking that many arguments; where more than one could take them, it answers as before (#1248)

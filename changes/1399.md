### Fixed

- `d` in Nix lands on the binding that holds at the cursor: a `let` or lambda closed earlier on the line, or one opened later on it, no longer takes the name, and a `rec { }` set's own attribute wins over an outer binding of its name (#1399)
- `d` in Starlark and Python lands on the binding that holds at the cursor's column: a one-line `def f(x): return x`, `def f(x = 2): return x` or `def f(): x = 1; return x` gives its parameter or local, a comprehension or lambda gives its own variable inside it, and no longer takes a namesake read beside it on the line, as in `[x for x in xs] + [x]` (#1399)
